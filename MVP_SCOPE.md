# MVP Scope and Pilot

## What this prototype proves
- Customers can understand appointment-based service discovery, inspect scope, and finish a simulated booking.
- Providers can understand posting an unused schedule opening and creating customer-visible appointment inventory.
- The concept can be demonstrated coherently to prospective users, providers, and partners.

## What it does not prove
It does not validate real demand or provider supply, marketplace liquidity, payment or insurance viability, legal compliance, verification reliability, route-optimization accuracy, unit economics, operational load, or safety outcomes. Local mock interactions are usability evidence only.

## Recommended pilot
Run a **4–8 week Cincinnati and Northern Kentucky pilot** across 1–3 adjacent ZIP codes with 5–10 lawn-care providers, 50–100 invited customers, and a few standardized packages. Manually approve every provider and opening exception; manually review disputes; keep support hours explicit; and avoid regulated categories. Begin with concierge scheduling/payment links only after legal, tax, payment, and insurance review.

## Pilot metrics
Track openings posted, percent booked, median time to booking, search-to-book conversion, average order value, provider net earnings, customer repeat rate, provider repeat-posting rate, customer/provider cancellation rates, average travel distance, dispute rate, customer satisfaction, and provider satisfaction. Segment by ZIP, package, lead time, and provider; define each metric before launch and pair conversion with safety/quality guardrails.
